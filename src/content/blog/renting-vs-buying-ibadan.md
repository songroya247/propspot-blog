Renting and buying in Ibadan look like opposite choices, but the decision usually comes down to three things: how long you plan to stay, how much cash you have upfront, and how much risk you're comfortable carrying.

## The upfront cost gap is bigger than people expect

A typical rental in a decent Ibadan neighborhood asks for one to two years upfront, plus agency and agreement fees that can add another 20-25% on top. Buying, on the other hand, means a much larger single outlay — but it's the last major housing payment you'll make for years.

## When renting makes more sense

- You expect to relocate within 2–3 years
- You want flexibility to test a neighborhood before committing
- Your cash is better used growing a business or investment right now

## When buying makes more sense

- You've identified a neighborhood you're confident in long-term
- You have land or property documentation checked and verified
- The monthly-equivalent cost of ownership is lower than what you'd pay in rent over the same period

## A practical middle ground

Some buyers start with land in an up-and-coming but affordable area — Ojoo, Apata, or parts of Akobo — and build gradually, avoiding the full cost of a finished home upfront while still building equity.

Whichever path you choose, always verify documentation before paying anything. A cheap plot with disputed title is not a bargain.
